Rhode Island

She Ran Against “Know a Guy.” Her Transition Is Led by Raimondo Alumni

Virtue Forge briefing · Nov. 9, 2026 · Based on the Boston Globe (primary-night speech), Rhode Island Current, WPRI 12, and helenatransition.com

What happened

On primary night, Sept. 9, Governor-elect Helena Buonanno Foulkes told Rhode Islanders the old State House cliché was finished. In remarks prepared for delivery and published by the Boston Globe, she said: “For too long, the cliché in this state has been that you gotta know a guy to get something done. Those days are gone.” She added: “Decisions in this state should get made by what’s right, not by who you know.”

Two days after she won the general election, her transition named its first leadership team and opened helenatransition.com for ideas and job applications. The named leaders, posted on the transition’s team page and reported by Rhode Island Current and WPRI 12, are: Caitlin Molina, executive director; RJ Kermes, deputy director; Jon Romano, senior adviser; Jon Carvalho, communications director; and Elisabeth Wales and Anthony Aquino, public engagement liaisons.

Who they are. Rhode Island Current reports that the roster “includes several familiar faces in Rhode Island politics, including senior staffers for former Gov. Gina Raimondo.” It identifies Molina as having worked in the Department of Human Services under Raimondo, and Romano as someone who “advised Raimondo during her time in office” and advised Foulkes’ campaign. Kermes, the outlet says, formerly served as chief of staff to Central Falls Mayor Maria Rivera. Carvalho previously worked as press secretary for New Bedford Mayor Jon Mitchell. WPRI reports five of the six had been working on the Foulkes campaign; Carvalho is the exception.

Foulkes’ transition statement, carried on her site and by the outlets above, says: “Rhode Islanders know what’s working, what isn’t, and what could be better. Whether you have a big idea or a simple fix, I want to hear it. We’re building a team to get results for Rhode Island, and the more we hear from people across our state, the better our plans for 2027 and beyond can be.” A full transition committee “will be announced in the coming days,” her team said.

Context. Rhode Island Current reported Monday that Office of Management and Budget Director Brian Daniels is resigning effective Dec. 11, leaving another cabinet seat for Foulkes to fill. Transition spokesperson Jon Carvalho told the outlet the OMB job “is a priority position for the Governor-elect to fill,” and that the transition had already received more than 200 applications.

Fact check: (1) The “know a guy” lines are from Foulkes’ Sept. 9 prepared victory remarks as published by the Boston Globe, not from a post-election interview. (2) The six names and titles match helenatransition.com’s team page. Raimondo ties for Molina and Romano are Rhode Island Current’s reporting. (3) Naming experienced staff is not illegal and is common in transitions; the news is the contrast with her own anti-insider pitch. (4) Election-night vote totals cited by outlets remain unofficial until the Board of Elections certifies. (5) A full transition committee had not been named as of the Nov. 9 briefing; it was posted Nov. 10 (see update).

Update, Nov 10, 2026

The full transition committee promised in those earlier materials was named Tuesday and posted on the transition’s team page. Bari Harlam and Central Falls Mayor Maria Rivera are listed as transition chairs. The site then names 21 committee members, for a 23-person roster that matches reporting by WPRI 12 and the Valley Breeze.

Among the names on the official list are David Cicilline (Rhode Island Foundation); Sen. Lou DiPalma and Rep. Nathan W. Biah Sr.; Johnston Mayor Joe Polisena; Pat Crowley (Rhode Island AFL-CIO); Danny Bianco (LIUNA); and Jesse Martin (SEIU Rhode Island State Council), along with business, nonprofit, housing, and health-care figures. Titles here follow helenatransition.com. GoLocalProv described the announcement as “loaded with Democratic politicians and union leaders.” The Valley Breeze, citing a news release, says committee members will meet in November and December “to make strategic recommendations to the governor-elect and her team.”

WJAR reports that Rivera said: “An important part of building a team is making sure people from different backgrounds, cultures, races, generations, and different walks of life are represented.” Foulkes, WJAR says, told reporters more than 700 Rhode Islanders have sent ideas and feedback, and that “Now we need to start delivering real results for people.”

Fact check: The 23-person count is two chairs plus 21 members as listed on helenatransition.com on Nov. 11. Officeholder and union titles in the second paragraph are taken from that page, not inferred. Cicilline’s current job is listed there only as “Rhode Island Foundation”; WJAR and the Valley Breeze separately identify him as the foundation’s president and CEO and a former Providence mayor and congressman. Naming elected officials and union officers to a transition committee is not illegal; the news is the contrast with Foulkes’ “know a guy” pitch and with fact-check item (5) below, which is now out of date.

Update, Nov 11, 2026: Ahern’s transition too

Attorney General-elect Kim Ahern named her transition committee on Tuesday, Nov. 10. WPRI 12 and the Providence Journal list Kevin Gallagher among the members and identify him as a former senior adviser to U.S. Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo; the Journal also notes he was senior deputy chief of staff to Raimondo. GoLocalProv, describing the same person, writes: “Today, he is a Rhode Island State House lobbyist.”

The Secretary of State’s Lobby Tracker lists a registered lobbyist named Kevin Gallagher, badge 14203, at 239 Cedar St., Warwick, RI 02818. For the 2026 session the registry shows a relationship with IGT a Nevada Company through Cedar Hill Consulting, LLC, from Jan. 9, 2026, through Dec. 31, 2026, at $25,000 annually. A 2025 relationship for the same entity and firm is also posted (Sept. 12–Dec. 31, 2025, $5,000 monthly).

Board of Elections campaign-finance records show two $2,000 contributions to Ahern from a Kevin Gallagher listed at 239 Cedar St., 02818, the same street address the Secretary of State’s lobby registry lists for lobbyist Kevin Gallagher (registry city: Warwick; BOE city: East Greenwich).

Fact check: Of the three news outlets, only GoLocal used the word “lobbyist.” The lobbyist status is confirmed in the Secretary of State’s registry, which does not itself mention Raimondo on the profile we reviewed. The registry lists the city as Warwick; the March 4 Board of Elections detail lists East Greenwich. Both records use 239 Cedar St. and ZIP 02818. Serving on a transition committee while registered as a lobbyist is not, by itself, illegal. We report only what these records show.

Virtue Forge commentary

Foulkes sold voters a simple promise: you should not need an insider to get a fair shake at the State House. Her first public staffing move puts two Raimondo-era hands at the center of the handoff. That may be competent government. It is not a clean break from the “who you know” machine she ran against.

Voters can hold both thoughts. Experience matters, and a governor-elect who has never held elective office needs people who know the levers. The test is whether helenatransition.com becomes a real door for outsiders, or a suggestion box next to a Rolodex that already knew the way in. The OMB vacancy and the rest of the cabinet will show which theory wins.

Bias check: Virtue Forge is skeptical of Rhode Island’s insider networks and takes Foulkes at her primary-night word. To be fair, Rhode Island Current notes she has never held elected office, five of six early transition staffers came off her own campaign, and every modern governor leans on people who have worked in government before. The issue is the gap between the slogan and the first names on the letterhead.

What to watch

• Cabinet picks, especially OMB and transportation, and whether the new Leadership Transition Committee steers those names or merely ratifies them.
• Whether outside applicants from helenatransition.com get real interviews, or the shortlists stay inside the usual circles.
• How Foulkes defines “fresh experience” once the know-a-guy test meets a committee that already includes legislative leaders, mayors, and union officers.
