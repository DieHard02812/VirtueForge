From Hospital Lobby to the Legislature’s Checkbook: Speaker Picks HARI’s Registered Lobbyist—a Donor to Three of the Five Leaders She’ll Answer To—to Run JCLS

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on Speaker Blazejewski’s Oct. 6, 2026 announcement (as posted by the Boston Globe’s Edward Fitzpatrick, the Providence Journal’s Katherine Gregg and Ocean State Media’s Ian Donnis), the Senate leadership statement, GoLocalProv (Oct. 7, 2026), the Secretary of State’s lobbying registry and Tomasso’s 2026 lobbyist reports, and HARI’s IRS Form 990 via ProPublica

What happened

On Tuesday, Oct. 6, House Speaker Christopher Blazejewski, who chairs the Joint Committee on Legislative Services (JCLS), announced that former Coventry Rep. Lisa P. Tomasso will become the committee’s executive director. She replaces Henry Kinch Jr., who, according to the announcement, “is retiring in early November.” JCLS isn’t a policy committee. According to the General Assembly’s own description, its office handles the legislature’s personnel, payroll and benefits, purchasing and accounts payable, prepares the legislature’s annual budget, and “oversees the finances of the Legislature.” Ocean State Media’s Ian Donnis called it “the hiring and spending arm of the legislature.”

Tomasso is coming straight from the industry side of the State House. The announcement identifies her as senior vice president of the Hospital Association of Rhode Island (HARI), where she “led government relations, public policy, and strategic communications” for the trade group representing 17 hospitals. She served in the House from 2011 to 2014. “We worked together as legislative colleagues,” Blazejewski said, “and I saw firsthand her knowledge of public policy and dedication to public service.” Senate President Valarie Lawson, Majority Leader Frank Ciccone, Majority Whip David Tikoian and Republican Minority Leader Jessica de la Cruz issued a joint welcome. It said they had “worked closely with Ms. Tomasso” during her time at HARI, where she “brought tremendous insight to the table as the Senate focused on improving our state’s health care system.”

The public record fills in what the announcement left out. On the Secretary of State’s lobbying registry, Lisa P. Tomasso (badge 12090) is a registered lobbyist for HARI for the 2026 session, with the registration running Jan. 1 through Dec. 31, 2026 at a reported $88.94 an hour. In each of her January–June 2026 reports, the officials she lists lobbying include “House Speaker,” “President of the Senate” and “House and Senate leadership,” along with the governor’s health and budget officials. Those same reports list 27 campaign contributions totaling $5,150 from January through June. Among them: $350 to Friends of Chris Blazejewski ($150 on Jan. 27 and $200 on May 29, three weeks after he became Speaker), $450 to Friends of Val Lawson, and $275 to Friends of Katherine Kazarian, now House majority leader. Blazejewski, Lawson and Kazarian are three of the five JCLS members. The other two, Republicans Michael Chippendale and Jessica de la Cruz, got nothing in these reports. The largest recipient was Gov. Dan McKee at $1,250.

HARI’s IRS Form 990 for the fiscal year ending September 2025 lists Tomasso (as “VP of Strategy & Public Re”) with $194,928 in reportable compensation and $20,301 in other compensation. Neither the Speaker’s announcement nor the Senate statement gave her state salary or an exact start date. For scale: when Speaker Shekarchi hired Kinch in 2021, the Providence Journal reported that his predecessor, Frank Montanaro, had been paid $167,948 in the job, and that the office oversaw about $46 million in legislative hiring and spending.

Fact check: This was not a single-outlet story. GoLocalProv’s Oct. 7 headline says Blazejewski “knocks” Kinch out, but the announcement itself, posted Oct. 6 by reporters at the Globe, ProJo and Ocean State Media, and the Senate leaders’ statement both call it a retirement, effective early November. We found no evidence that Kinch was pushed, and no public comment from him, so the “ouster” framing is unconfirmed. The “senior vice president” title comes from the announcement and HARI’s staff page. The 990 still lists her older VP title for that fiscal year. The $194,928 is HARI pay for FY2025, not her JCLS salary, which hasn’t been disclosed. The $88.94 hourly figure and the per-paycheck “compensation” amounts in the lobby reports are the share of her HARI pay attributed to lobbying, not her full salary. The contributions are small, legal and publicly disclosed, and they cover only January through June. Nothing illegal is alleged. We found no public statement that she will end her HARI lobbying registration or recuse herself from hospital-related matters.

Virtue Forge commentary

Last week this site noted that McKee’s health chief of staff, Daniel Connors, had been named HARI’s executive director, with a Monday, Oct. 5 start. On Tuesday, the door swung the other way. HARI’s senior vice president for government relations, still a registered lobbyist this year whose filings list the Speaker and Senate President as officials she lobbied, was named to run the office that controls the legislature’s hiring, payroll, purchasing and budget. In one week the hospital lobby took in a top health official from the executive branch and sent its chief advocate into the legislature’s back office.

JCLS is where legislative power gets centralized. The Speaker chairs it. Its director runs hiring, firing, payroll and purchasing for the whole General Assembly, an office the public sees little of. Rhode Island leaders keep that office close to the chair, and this pick is no exception. The Speaker chose a former House colleague and a donor to his own campaign this year, and two other leaders on the committee received her checks too. It may be perfectly legal. It is still the kind of insider loop a self-governing people ought to be uneasy about: the people who lobby the leadership one month run the leadership’s payroll the next.

Bias check: Tomasso has real credentials: four years as a legislator, years managing a statewide association, and praise from both parties, including GOP Senate Minority Leader Jessica de la Cruz. JCLS is an administrative job, not a policy-writing one, and hiring someone who knows how the building works is defensible. Lobbyists giving a few hundred dollars to leaders is routine and disclosed in Rhode Island. The real question is not her competence. It is whether the public gets the basics a private employer would demand: her salary, her start date, her end date at HARI, and a written recusal from anything touching hospitals for a cooling-off period.

What to watch: whether JCLS posts the salary and start date, whether Tomasso’s HARI lobbying registration ends before she starts in November, whether she files a recusal or seeks an Ethics Commission advisory opinion on hospital-related matters, and whether HARI names a new government-relations chief.
