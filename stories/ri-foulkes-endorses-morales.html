Big Tent for Municipal Socialism: Foulkes Will Endorse Providence’s DSA Mayor

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on WJAR / NBC 10 (Brian Crandall, Oct 2, 2026) and prior on-the-record Providence primary coverage

What happened

Democratic gubernatorial nominee Helena Foulkes told NBC 10 on Friday, Oct 2, 2026, that she will vote for and would endorse democratic socialist David Morales in the Providence mayor’s race. The comments came during a recording of 10 News Conference.

Foulkes, who lives in Providence, defeated Gov. Dan McKee in the September Democratic primary. Morales beat incumbent Mayor Brett Smiley in the Sept. 9 Democratic mayoral primary and is the heavy favorite against Republican Dave Talan on Nov. 3. WJAR reported that Smiley will not endorse Morales.

Asked about Democratic concern over democratic socialists, Foulkes said, “I think we have to be a big tent party.” On voting for Morales: “Yes, I will.” On endorsing him: “Yes. I’m a Democrat and he is, too.” She also said she thinks she and Morales “will have a really good relationship” if both win, and that she wants to be “a governor who is supporting Providence and him.”

The same interview underscores a policy split that WJAR flagged explicitly: Foulkes opposes rent control, as Smiley did, while rent control was a central issue in the mayoral primary that Morales won. Separately, Foulkes said she is focused on housing costs and wants to use Rhode Island’s new millionaire’s tax to fund 20,000 new units—a timeline McKee had claimed would take too long.

Fact check: Do not claim Foulkes supports rent control—WJAR states she opposes it. Do not invent a formal written endorsement packet or joint campaign event; the sourcing here is her on-camera “Yes” answers to NBC 10 about voting and endorsing. Morales is not yet mayor; he is the Democratic nominee after beating Smiley. The “20,000 new units” figure is Foulkes’s stated use of the millionaire’s tax in that interview, not an enacted appropriation. Prior Virtue Forge briefings document Morales’s DSA affiliation and allies’ “municipal socialism” framing; this page does not re-litigate those primary-night claims as if they were new Friday news.

Virtue Forge commentary

This is not a horse-race poll story. It is the Democratic nominee for governor of Rhode Island telling voters she will put her name behind Providence’s democratic-socialist mayor-in-waiting—after Smiley, the Democrat Morales beat, refused to do the same. “Big tent” is the soft word. The hard fact is party consolidation around a City Hall project that ran on price controls and social housing.

Bias check: Foulkes is free to endorse any Democrat she chooses. Pointing out the contradiction is not inventing hypocrisy: she says she opposes rent control and will still endorse the candidate for whom rent control was central. That is her own pairing of positions, reported by WJAR. Likewise, wanting to steer a new millionaire’s tax into 20,000 state-directed housing units is her stated plan—not a Virtue Forge invention. Markets build housing when rules and costs allow; bonding and taxing “the rich” to conjure units is the statist alternative Rhode Island keeps choosing.

Civic virtue asks for clarity, not branding. If Foulkes believes rent caps hurt supply, saying so while elevating the rent-cap nominee is not competence—it is coalition management. Rhode Islanders who want trustworthy government should hear the endorsement and the millionaire-tax housing bet as one package: statewide Democrats folding municipal socialism into the tent, then asking taxpayers to fund the housing arithmetic the private market is told not to solve.
