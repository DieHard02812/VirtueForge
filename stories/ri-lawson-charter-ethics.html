Union Boss With the Gavel: Rhode Island Ethics Panel Clears Senate President After Freezing Charter School Competition

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on Boston Globe reporting (Sept. 1, 2026)

What happened

On Sept. 1, 2026, the Rhode Island Ethics Commission voted 4–1 to dismiss a complaint against Senate President Valarie J. Lawson. Lawson also serves as president of the National Education Association Rhode Island (NEARI). The complaint, filed by Defending Education, argued she should have recused from votes on a three-year moratorium on new charter schools (and a related card-check unionization bill) because of her dual role.

Commissioners concluded the complaint did not state facts that, if true, would constitute an ethics-code violation. Lawson said she voted consistent with prior commission guidance. She had recused on some other matters during the session.

Virtue Forge commentary

Whatever the formal ethics outcome, the policy substance remains: Rhode Island froze new charter competition while the Senate’s presiding officer also leads a teachers union that has long opposed charter expansion. Families lose options; incumbents in the district system keep a protected franchise.

Bias check: Coverage that treats this mainly as “complaint dismissed, move along” skips the conflict-of-interest optics voters can see without a law degree. Calling Defending Education “out-of-state” or “Koch-funded” is a classic motive smear—it does not answer whether one person should set the agenda on both sides of the labor–school-choice fight.

Free enterprise in education means allowing new schools to compete for students. A moratorium is industrial policy for the status quo. Pro-American civic culture depends on honest rules and competent delivery—not gatekeeping by the same interests that staff the legislature’s leadership.

If ethics rules formally permit the dual role, that is a reason to tighten the rules—not to pretend the arrangement looks trustworthy.
