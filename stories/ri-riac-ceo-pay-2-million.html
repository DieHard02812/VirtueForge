Rhode Island

A State Airport Agency Just Doubled Its CEO’s Pay to $2.19 Million a Year. Massport’s Boss Makes $481,000

Virtue Forge briefing · Nov. 13, 2026 · Based on NBC 10 / WJAR I-Team (Nov. 13, 2026), GoLocalProv (Nov. 13, 2026) and RIAC’s published 2025 employment agreement

What happened

On Thursday, Nov. 12, the board of the Rhode Island Airport Corporation (RIAC), the quasi-public agency that runs T.F. Green International and the state’s five smaller airports, voted unanimously to approve a new three-year contract for President and CEO Iftikhar Ahmad. NBC 10’s I-Team reports the deal is retroactive to Oct. 13, his 10th anniversary in the job, and that it more than doubles his annual compensation.

According to NBC 10’s breakdown of the contract:

• Base salary rises to $687,760.
• A maximum bonus of 20% of pay: $137,552.
• $212,585 a year “with the intention, although not the requirement, of funding an annuity to support Employee after retirement.”
• $55,020 in retirement pay that RIAC spokesman Bill Fischer said is not in the contract.
• On top of that, “retention compensation” of $546,459 every six months he stays, or $1,092,918 a year.

Total: $2,185,834 a year. A year ago, NBC 10 reported, Ahmad signed a deal with a base salary just under $528,000 and a total payout of around $900,000. RIAC’s own published 2025 agreement lists that base salary as $527,728.39.

The contract also includes 28 vacation days (up to two weeks can be cashed out), eight personal days and 24 sick days that carry over without limit and are paid out if unused, which NBC 10 calculates as up to 12 paid weeks off a year, plus a vehicle for unlimited use with maintenance included.

Board Chairman Jonathan Savage said in a statement that the package “is reflective of compensation in the airport sector based on an independent study, rewards him for his accomplishments and ensures we retain his services.” The board’s resolution credits Ahmad with a “fundamental financial turnaround,” and GoLocalProv published parts of it citing liquidity growing from about $30.7 million in 2016 to $111 million in 2025, total debt falling from about $311.4 million to $186.5 million, and nonstop destinations rising from 17 to 40.

Fact check: does RIAC’s own study back a $2.19 million package? Not by NBC 10’s reading. The station reports that the compensation report RIAC made public shows no other leader of a small-sized airport (the category it lists T.F. Green in) making more than about $920,000, with most well below that. Leaders of two medium-sized airports made just over $1 million. For a regional comparison, NBC 10 notes that Massport’s CEO, who runs Boston Logan, recently got a raise to $481,000 plus a 10% bonus. RIAC points out that most other airports give their CEO a defined pension and RIAC doesn’t. That explains the annuity line. It doesn’t explain a second $1.09 million a year in “retention” money.
Context: “No taxpayer funds”? NBC 10 reports that the pay comes from airport revenues, and GoLocalProv says RIAC is not part of the state budget and gets no state funding. That’s true as far as the state budget goes. But RIAC’s own 2025 contract with Ahmad describes the corporation as “a governmental agency and public instrumentality of the State of Rhode Island,” and the contract names him the state’s Director of Aviation. Airport revenue is money paid by travelers, airlines and tenants at a public airport. It isn’t a private company’s profit.

Virtue Forge commentary

By the numbers RIAC itself cites, Ahmad has done good work. Credit where due: more routes, less debt, better credit ratings and national awards. A well-run public airport is worth paying for, and nobody should begrudge a competent executive a fair raise.

But a $2.19 million package isn’t a fair raise. It’s what happens when a public body answers to nobody. RIAC sits outside the state budget, so no legislator had to vote for this package and no governor had to sign it. An appointed board approved it unanimously, praised the CEO in a formal resolution, and leaned on a study that, according to NBC 10, doesn’t show any comparable airport chief anywhere near this figure. Then it added more than $1 million a year in “retention” pay on top of a package that was already above $1 million.

Bias check: GoLocalProv’s write-up opened with the airport’s accomplishments and mostly ran RIAC’s release and resolution; its compensation figure was corrected after publication. NBC 10 did the arithmetic and checked the board’s own study. Read both, but the I-Team’s numbers come straight from the contract.

This is the quasi-public model working as designed: public power, public assets and public fees, run with private-sector pay and very little public accountability. Rhode Island has a long list of these agencies. If the board believes Ahmad is worth more than twice what Massport pays to run Logan, it should publish the full contract and the full study, and explain to the airlines and travelers who pay these fees why retaining one executive is worth $1 million a year on its own.

What to watch: whether the General Assembly or the incoming administration asks RIAC to justify the retention payments, and whether the board releases the full independent compensation study.
