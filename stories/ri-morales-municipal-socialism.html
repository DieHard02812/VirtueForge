Laboratory for Municipal Socialism: Morales Allies Spell Out Providence’s Mandate

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on GoLocalProv exclusive video, WPRI post-primary coverage, and Boston Globe / Rhode Island Current debate reporting

What happened

After State Rep. David Morales, a democratic socialist, defeated Mayor Brett Smiley in Providence’s Sept. 9, 2026 Democratic mayoral primary, WPRI on Sept. 11 revisited what a democratic-socialist City Hall would mean in practice.

During the campaign, Reclaim RI co-chair and podcast host Dan Denvir told a New York Morales fundraiser crowd—captured in a GoLocalProv exclusive video—that “David is going to turn Providence into a laboratory for municipal socialism,” with Morales present and applauding, per Boston Globe / Rhode Island Current debate coverage of the episode. In a later WPRI interview, Denvir framed “municipal socialism” as Milwaukee-style “sewer socialism”—high-quality public services—and said corporate interests “have a lot to be afraid of.” Post-primary, Denvir told WPRI the biggest shift would be housing: social housing, cooperatives, and tenant protections from the “private real estate market.”

In a July WPRI debate, Morales rejected the “laboratory” word—“We will not be a laboratory of any sort”—while affirming a working-people / affordability agenda; Smiley attacked the lab framing as experimenting on residents. Morales is the heavy favorite against Republican Dave Talan on Nov. 3; a progressive council majority is already advancing a rent-cap agenda (covered separately on Virtue Forge).

Fact check: Separate Denvir’s on-the-record “laboratory for municipal socialism” slogan from Morales’s debate walk-back of the word “laboratory.” Both are on the record; one does not erase the other.

Virtue Forge commentary

Soft branding as “fairness” doesn’t erase the hard project: price controls, social housing, and a governing coalition that says corporate interests should fear City Hall.

Bias check: Smiley’s fearmongering was campaign politics; Denvir’s own words and Morales’s DSA affiliation are the checkable core. Morales’s debate denial of “laboratory” is spin control, not a renunciation of rent caps or social housing.

Markets and property rights fund the tax base that pays for those “excellent public services.” Treating Providence as a lab for municipal socialism is leftist centralization of economic power—exactly the Virtue Forge lane.
