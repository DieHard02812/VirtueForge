‘Free College,’ Paid For by Pell: RI Democratic Party Chair’s Husband Indicted in $20M Ohio Racketeering Case

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on GoLocalProv (Oct. 19, 2026), the Ohio Auditor of State’s Oct. 8, 2026 release and the Jefferson County, Ohio indictment, with background from The Providence Journal (Nov. 2023)

What happened

GoLocalProv reported Monday that Michael Perik, husband of Rhode Island Democratic Party chair Elizabeth “Liz” Beretta-Perik, is one of three people indicted in Ohio in an alleged scheme to divert about $20 million from Eastern Gateway Community College, a public two-year college in Steubenville that has since closed.

According to the Ohio Auditor of State, a Jefferson County grand jury returned a 24-count racketeering indictment, announced Oct. 8, against Perik; Nicole Rowe Colclasure, president and part owner of Perik’s Student Resource Center (SRC); and Michael Geoghegan, the college’s former CFO and president. Perik and Colclasure each face a dozen felony counts, including engaging in a pattern of corrupt activities, aggravated theft of $1.5 million or more, bribery, telecommunications fraud, money laundering, receiving stolen property and having an unlawful interest in a public contract.

The state alleges that SRC ran a “Free College Benefit Program” for union members and their families. Under its 2017 contract, the college reimbursed SRC’s costs and then paid it 50% of the surplus revenue, which included tuition, state subsidy and Pell Grants. Prosecutors say the “scholarships” were presented as union-funded but were in fact largely paid for by Pell Grants meant for needy students plus large tuition write-offs at taxpayers’ expense. They also allege that Perik’s companies were not the independent third parties federal law requires and instead controlled enrollment, attendance verification and access to financial aid, which made the profit share illegal incentive compensation.

From 2017 to March 2022, the auditor says, enrollment grew from about 8,500 to more than 60,000, more than 90% online and about 80% out of state. The U.S. Department of Education’s final program review found more than $44.4 million in liabilities and projected more than $259 million after the college closed without the required records.

Perik’s Ohio attorney, Brad Barbin, told GoLocal there “was no crime or lack of transparency.” He said the college’s operations were reviewed for a decade by state and federal agencies, that the Justice Department agreed in 2024 to dismiss a federal court action, and that Perik “followed all the protocols and received sign-offs from agency lawyers.” He called the case “purely political payback.” GoLocal reports that Liz Beretta-Perik is not implicated.

The Periks live in Jamestown. The Providence Journal reported in 2023, when Beretta-Perik became chair as House Speaker Joe Shekarchi’s handpicked choice, that each of them had given more than $100,000 to Rhode Island politicians. GoLocal now puts the couple’s giving to local Democrats at more than $270,000. Before becoming chair, she was the party’s treasurer and finance chair for Attorney General Peter Neronha from 2018 to 2023.

Fact check: (1) These are allegations. No one has been convicted, and Perik denies wrongdoing. (2) GoLocal’s headline says “$20M in Pell Grants.” The auditor describes about $20 million in Pell Grants, other federal aid and Ohio state subsidies combined, not $20 million in Pell money alone. (3) GoLocal says the college had “more than 15,000 students.” The auditor says enrollment topped 60,000 during the period charged. (4) The case is an Ohio state prosecution brought by special prosecutors from the auditor’s office, announced alongside a Trump administration education official. That is the factual basis for the defense’s “political payback” claim, but a grand jury returned the indictment, and the Education Department’s own review found the aid problems. (5) The $270,000 donation total is GoLocal’s figure. Virtue Forge has not independently totaled the Board of Elections records.

Update (Oct 20): RI Democratic Party Chair Resigns a Day After Saying Indictment Had ‘Nothing to Do’ With Her

Liz Beretta-Perik has resigned as chair of the Rhode Island Democratic Party. Party executive director Katherine Riordan confirmed the resignation to Rhode Island Current on Tuesday. RI Current reports that she sent a one-sentence email to Rep. Arthur Corvese, a North Providence Democrat and secretary of the state committee, at 12:22 p.m. WPRI reports she emailed the party’s executive committee and said the resignation was effective immediately. On Monday, according to RI Current, she had said her husband’s indictment had “nothing to do” with her or her role as party leader.

Who called for it. Democratic gubernatorial nominee Helena Foulkes called on her to resign Monday night: “These are serious and concerning allegations. While everyone is entitled to due process, we are weeks away from a pivotal election that will determine the future of our state for years to come. I thank Liz for her service, but in the best interest of the future of Rhode Island, she should step aside at this time.” Michael Perik gave Foulkes $2,000 during her 2022 campaign. Foulkes spokesperson Angelika Pellegrino said the campaign is “in the process” of donating Perik’s contributions to a scholarship at the Community College of Rhode Island. Republican nominee Aaron Guckian also called for her resignation. Independent Ken Block said he would “leave it up to Democrats.”

Who defended her. An hour before he learned of the resignation, Democratic national committeeman Joe Paolino Jr. told RI Current: “This is her husband, not her. Why get penalized by her spouse?” After she resigned, House Speaker Christopher Blazejewski and Senate President Valarie Lawson thanked her for her “strong leadership and dedication to the Rhode Island Democratic Party over the last four years,” WPRI reported, and said her “resignation should not take away from the integrity and professionalism she always applied to her work on behalf of Rhode Island Democrats.”

Fact check: (1) These are not federal charges. RI Current’s headline and many posts on X describe “federal fraud charges.” The indictment was returned by a Jefferson County, Ohio grand jury in a state prosecution brought by the Ohio Auditor of State’s special prosecutors. It has 24 counts in total, 12 of them against Perik. Some X posts also say “20 counts,” which is wrong. The money at issue includes federal student aid, which is likely where the confusion comes from. (2) She has not been charged. Beretta-Perik is not named or charged, and Perik denies wrongdoing. (3) Accounts of the email differ. RI Current says it went to Corvese; WPRI says it went to the executive committee. Both say she resigned Tuesday.

Update (Oct 21): Speaker Hand-Picks Freshman Rep. Jenni Furtado as Party Chair; Committee to Ratify Later

The Speaker has named the replacement. House Speaker Christopher Blazejewski announced Wednesday that Rep. Jenni Furtado, an East Providence Democrat, will be the next chair of the Rhode Island Democratic Party, replacing Liz Beretta-Perik. Furtado is 33 and a freshman, first elected in 2024 to House District 64, which covers parts of Pawtucket and East Providence. She is unopposed on Nov. 3, according to Rhode Island Current.

Blazejewski said in a statement: “These are difficult times, and Democrats need a chair who knows what working families are up against. Jenni does, and I’m grateful she’s willing to take this on.” Furtado thanked him in her own statement: “I’m honored by the opportunity to serve as chairwoman of the Rhode Island Democratic Party, and I’m grateful to Speaker Blazejewski for his support.”

Announced first, ratified later. According to RI Current, the 150-member Democratic State Central Committee is expected to meet to ratify Furtado “in the coming days,” although the date and time have not been set. NBC 10 likewise reports that Blazejewski said the committee will meet to ratify her.

How it’s done in Rhode Island. RI Current notes that the House speaker has historically played a prominent role in picking party leaders. In 2014, then-Speaker Nicholas Mattiello backed Rep. Joe McNamara as chair after Rep. David Caprio resigned. The Providence Journal reported in 2023 that Beretta-Perik herself took the job as Speaker Joe Shekarchi’s choice.

Context: Nothing here is a knock on Furtado, who has no connection to the Perik case. The point is the process. The leader of one legislative chamber chose the leader of the state’s dominant party, which raises money for and campaigns for the same legislators who elect him speaker. The party’s own committee is left to approve the pick afterward. Three straight chairs have now come to the job with a speaker’s backing. The ratification vote has not happened yet.

Virtue Forge commentary

“Free college” is never free. Somebody pays, and in this case prosecutors say the somebody was low-income students whose Pell Grants were pooled to pay for a union perk, with a private middleman taking half of the surplus. That is the danger of subsidy programs dressed up as generosity: when public money flows through a “collaboration” with a profit share, the incentive is to maximize enrollment and aid, not learning. Enrollment going from 8,500 to more than 60,000, mostly online and out of state, should have set off alarms years earlier.

The Rhode Island angle is about the people who run the state’s one-party politics. The Democratic Party chair was installed without a contest as the Speaker’s pick, and her household is one of the party’s biggest funders. Rhode Islanders deserve to know whether candidates and the party will hold on to money from a fortune that Ohio now says was built in part on misdirected student aid. If Republicans were in this position, Rhode Island Democrats would ask exactly that question.

Bias check: Liz Beretta-Perik is not charged, and a spouse’s indictment is not her wrongdoing. Perik is presumed innocent, and his lawyer’s point that federal agencies reviewed and once dropped a case is a real defense argument to watch. The fair question for the party is about money and judgment, not guilt by marriage.

What to watch: Perik’s arraignment and plea in Jefferson County; whether the Rhode Island Democratic Party or Beretta-Perik comments; and whether Rhode Island candidates who took Perik donations return or hold them.
