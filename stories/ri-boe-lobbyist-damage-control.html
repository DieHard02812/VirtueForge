Outsourced Referee: After Excess Foulkes Donations Surfaced, RI’s Board of Elections Ran Its Response Through a Lobbying Firm Whose President Had Just Given Foulkes $1,000

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on GoLocalProv (Oct. 19, 2026, two reports), with Board of Elections campaign-finance records, WPRI (Sept. 2026), Rhode Island Current (Sept. 10, 2026) and Advocacy Solutions’ website

What happened

In late September, GoLocalProv reported that Democratic gubernatorial nominee Helena Foulkes’s campaign had accepted donations above Rhode Island’s $2,000-per-year individual limit. The Board of Elections (BOE), the state’s campaign-finance regulator, then expanded its own review. On Monday, GoLocal published emails and text messages it obtained through an Access to Public Records Act request. GoLocal says they show how the board handled the fallout: messaging went through Chris Hunter, a managing director of the State House lobbying and communications firm Advocacy Solutions, which GoLocal reports the board pays $250 an hour for strategic communications.

The chair’s texts. GoLocal attributes these texts to BOE Chair Jennie Johnson, sent in a group thread with Hunter and Executive Director Miguel Nuñez. They refer to Campaign Finance Director Richard “Ric” Thornton, who had been answering GoLocal’s questions. As GoLocal published them in its article:

“I have great respect for Ric [Thornton], but I am very concerned he spoke with GoLocal about an issue involving the governor’s race without consultation. We hear serious — and at times egregious — campaign finance matters in executive session.”

“I have been very clear with Ric that all commuincation [sic] with the media with the media [sic] first need to be consulted with Chris or his firm, even if it seems like an informal reach out.”

The screenshots GoLocal published also show this line: “For the BOE to comment publicly on a matter connected to an active gubernatorial race raises significant concerns. I would like to review any statement and correction before it goes out.”

The lobbyist in the enforcement loop. Other screenshots show Hunter texting a BOE staffer, whom GoLocal identifies as Thornton: “Honest mistake. You should speak with campaign and have Miguel with you.” In a separate thread, Nuñez writes “3pm conference call” / “With Foulkes,” and Hunter replies, “Ok, send me the link.” Later, Hunter asks, “9 instances of incorrect exceedence correct?” The staffer answers that he is “almost sure it’s 9” and that the color-coded portion of a spreadsheet “is BOE error.” GoLocal says neither Hunter nor Johnson would explain why an outside communications consultant needed to be on an enforcement call with a campaign.

Who’s who. According to GoLocal, Advocacy Solutions’ head, State House lobbyist Francis X. McMahon, gave Foulkes $1,000 about three weeks before GoLocal’s first story. GoLocal also reports that Hunter has given $53,335.14 to Rhode Island candidates, all but $250 of it to Democrats. GoLocal says Johnson was appointed to the board in 2018 under then-Gov. Gina Raimondo, to whose campaigns she had given $175.

The board’s response. Hunter did not answer GoLocal’s specific questions. He sent a statement saying that on Sept. 23 “Board of Elections staff provided GoLocalProv with incorrect information regarding campaign contribution exceedances for candidate Helena Foulkes”; that on Sept. 24 the Foulkes campaign asked for a correction and a meeting “to agree on the amount of exceedances that needed to be cured”; and that a corrected spreadsheet went to GoLocal and other outlets that day. “Local and statewide candidates may request meetings with Board staff,” the statement said. GoLocal says Johnson refused to answer its questions.

The calls to resign. In a second GoLocal story Monday, independent gubernatorial candidate Ken Block said, “Jennie Johnson should step down as Chair immediately.” He added: “Why was a taxpayer-funded communications consultant involving himself in an enforcement matter? Why did the Chair insist that the Board’s campaign finance expert clear his communications through that consultant? And why have the Board’s audits failed to uncover violations that reporters found?” Rhode Island GOP Chairman Allyn Meyers also called for Johnson’s resignation and an independent investigation: “Since when does an independent state agency outsource communications to politically connected lobbyists and consultants?”

Fact check / what we verified: (1) Verified in Board of Elections records (ERTS): a $1,000 contribution from Francis X. McMahon (employer: Advocacy Solutions LLC) to Helena Foulkes dated Sept. 1, 2026; a total of $53,335.14 in individual contributions for donor search “Hunter, Chris,” with all records we sampled listing Advocacy Solutions as employer; and two contributions to Raimondo from a Jennifer Johnson (employer: City Year) totaling $175, in 2017 and 2018. We did not check the party split of Hunter’s giving. (2) Verified elsewhere: Advocacy Solutions’ website lists McMahon as president (GoLocal calls him CEO) and Hunter as a managing director. (3) GoLocal-only: the $250/hour rate and the contract terms. We could not find the BOE contract in public procurement or transparency records. (4) The texts don’t match GoLocal’s transcript word for word. In the screenshot, the second message reads: “I have been very clear with Ric that all communications with the media first need to be consulted with Chris or his firm, even if it just seems like an informal reach out.” The typo and the repeated words appear only in GoLocal’s transcription. The screenshots are also cropped, and sender names aren’t visible on the chair’s messages. The second message appears as an outgoing message from the phone’s owner in a thread whose title lists “Chris Hunter, Jennie Johnson” as the other participants, and it begins, “I sent an email to the Board with an update half an hour ago.” That suggests it may have been sent by a staffer rather than Johnson. GoLocal attributes it to Johnson, and we cannot confirm the sender. (5) The audits. GoLocal says the BOE’s twice-a-year audits failed to catch the excess donations. But on Sept. 23, Thornton told GoLocal, “BOE audits identified most of the contributions exceeding the allowable limit published in your GoLocalProv article, as well as others not published in the article,” and said earlier violations had been flagged and were still unresolved. If he’s right, the failure was in enforcement and follow-through, not detection. (6) The donations. “Improper” is the right word. We have seen no BOE finding of a violation or a penalty. The Foulkes campaign told WPRI it had identified $10,500 to refund and attributed the problem to donors who share a name, married couples, and refunds already issued or coming. The BOE first put the excess at $34,445, then sent a corrected spreadsheet on Sept. 24. Hunter’s texts reference “9 instances” of BOE error. We have not seen a final cured total. (7) Hunter’s role wasn’t new. Rhode Island Current quoted Hunter as BOE spokesperson on Sept. 10, before GoLocal’s first story. GoLocal’s word “muted” is its own characterization. (8) Block and Meyers: we found no primary post or release, so their statements are attributed to GoLocal. GoLocal’s second story says Johnson “did respond to questions,” which appears to contradict its first story’s report that she refused to answer. It may be a typo.

Virtue Forge commentary

The Board of Elections is Rhode Island’s referee for campaign money. Its job is to count the excess donations, order the refunds and tell the public what happened, in a way that leaves no doubt the rules apply equally to everyone. A referee doesn’t hand the whistle to someone who works for the teams.

Advocacy Solutions is a State House lobbying shop. Its president had just written the nominee a $1,000 check, and its managing director is one of the state’s steadier Democratic donors. When a donation scandal touched the race for governor, that firm became the board’s voice. The texts show its man asking for the link to the enforcement call with the very campaign under review. In those same texts, the career official who actually knows campaign-finance law is told to clear his media contacts through the consultant first. Even if every decision was made honestly, the arrangement puts a political insider between the regulator and the public at exactly the wrong moment.

The fix isn’t complicated. Enforcement matters should be explained by the staff who handle them, on the record. Communications contracts should go to people who don’t lobby the legislature that funds the board or donate to the candidates it polices. The board should publish its Advocacy Solutions contract, invoices and scope of work, and say on the record whether Hunter took part in any enforcement discussion.

Bias check: GoLocal broke the original story, and the board’s response it is now criticizing was partly aimed at GoLocal’s own reporting. GoLocal has a stake here, and its headline language (“damage control,” “illegal”) runs ahead of any BOE finding. The board says its staff gave GoLocal incorrect numbers, and Hunter’s texts reference “BOE error,” which may be part of why the chair wanted statements reviewed. Hiring outside communications help is not improper in itself; plenty of public bodies do it, including, per GoLocal, Pawtucket with this same firm. Block is running against Foulkes, and Meyers chairs the opposing party. The fair question is about conflicts and judgment, not proof of favoritism. No one has shown that the outcome for Foulkes was changed.

What to watch: whether the board releases the Advocacy Solutions contract and invoices; any response from Johnson or the board at its next meeting; the final cured amount and any penalty in the Foulkes matter; and whether Thornton speaks on the record again.
