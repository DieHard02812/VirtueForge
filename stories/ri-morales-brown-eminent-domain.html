The City Approved It. Mayor-Elect Morales Says He’ll Look at Eminent Domain Anyway: Providence’s Next Mayor Floats Seizing Two Houses Brown Owns to Stop a New Building

Virtue Forge briefing · Nov. 19, 2026 · Based on GoLocalProv and Brown Daily Herald reporting, plus the Providence City Plan Commission staff report

What happened

On Tuesday, Nov. 17, the Providence City Plan Commission approved an amendment to Brown University’s Institutional Master Plan that clears the way for a new economics building at Brook and Charlesfield Streets. The Brown Daily Herald reported that “Three commissioners voted in favor of the amendment, and two voted against it.”

The day after the vote, Mayor-elect David Morales told GoLocalProv he would revisit the decision once he takes office: “When I take office in January, I will work with our Law Department to explore all options available following the City Plan Commission’s decision, including reviewing the legal and financial considerations around eminent domain.”

He added: “I remain deeply concerned about the community impacts of the Brook Street project. With Providence families struggling to find homes they can afford, we should be looking for ways to put neglected properties to use for our community. Brown has a responsibility to maintain its properties and address the impact of its expansion on housing and residents’ quality of life.”

This isn’t a new idea for him. In April, as a candidate, Morales said, according to GoLocal, that “The Mayor of Providence should exercise eminent domain to take back neglected properties and return them to use for our community,” and called Brown’s plan “a clear example of how they hoard both wealth and housing in our city.”

According to the city planning staff report, which recommended approval with conditions, the four-story, 56,000-square-foot building would bring together an economics department now spread across five buildings. Two houses, 277 and 281 Brook Street, are slated for demolition, “but both houses will be made available for purchase and relocation prior to demolition,” and if no one buys them they will be offered to local groups for architectural salvage. A third building, 75 Charlesfield Street, would be torn down, and 71 Charlesfield would be moved onto that lot.

Neighbors and preservationists objected at the meeting. Ward 1 Councilor John Goncalves praised Brown for cutting the number of affected houses from four to two, but he called on the university to save the remaining two, citing a Providence Preservation Society estimate that Brown has demolished about 150 buildings on College Hill since 1945. Brown’s attorney, Andrew Teitz, told the commission the overall benefit was “better than freezing it and sending this whole area into a stasis.”

Fact check: (1) “Historic homes.” GoLocal’s caption calls them historic homes. The city staff report says the buildings are not in “the College Hill National Historic Landmark District or the College Hill Local Historic District.” They may be old and handsome, but they aren’t in either protected district. (2) “Neglected properties.” That’s Morales’s description, and GoLocal has reported neighbor complaints about Brown’s vacant houses in the past. But the plan isn’t to leave them empty. Under the approved plan, the two Brook Street houses will be offered to anyone who wants to buy and move them before they come down. (3) Nothing has been seized. Morales takes office in January and has said only that he will “explore” options, including eminent domain. No taking has been proposed, filed, or voted on, and the city would have to pay fair value for anything it took.

Virtue Forge commentary

Brown followed the process. It filed the plan the city’s zoning law requires, cut the project from four houses to two after pushback, agreed to offer the remaining two for relocation, and won a vote of the city’s own planning commission. Providence’s incoming mayor’s response is that the rules produced the wrong answer, so he’ll look at whether the government can take the property instead.

Eminent domain is supposed to be the narrowest power government has: taking private land, with payment, for a genuine public use like a road or a school. Morales is talking about using it to overrule a lawful land-use approval because he and some neighbors don’t like what a private owner plans to build on its own lot. If a mayor can threaten a taking every time a project he dislikes clears the commission, every property owner in the city, not just a university, is on notice that an approval in Providence is only good until the next election.

And taxpayers would pay for it. A taking requires paying fair value for East Side real estate, plus legal costs if Brown fights, which it almost certainly would. That money would come from a city that already has a long list of bills. Morales’s own phrasing, that Brown “hoards” wealth and housing, makes the motive clear. This is about leverage over an institution he’s been attacking since the campaign, not about two houses.

Bias check: Virtue Forge favors property rights and is skeptical of using government to override them. To be fair, neighbors’ concerns about Brown’s steady expansion into Fox Point are real, the commission split 3-2, and the university is a tax-exempt institution with enormous influence over the city. Morales hasn’t done anything yet beyond promising a legal review. Our objection is to treating a seizure as a fallback whenever a lawful approval doesn’t go the way City Hall wants.

What to watch

• Whether the Morales administration asks the Law Department for a formal eminent-domain review after he takes office in January, and what it estimates the cost would be.
• Whether anyone buys and moves 277 or 281 Brook Street before demolition.
• Whether neighbors appeal the commission’s decision.
