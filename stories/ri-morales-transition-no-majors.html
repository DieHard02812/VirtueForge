Morales Names 135 Transition Aides — Still No One From Providence’s Largest Employers or Universities

Virtue Forge briefing · Nov. 6, 2026 · Based on GoLocalProv and The Providence Eye

What happened

Providence Mayor-elect David Morales announced Thursday the membership of nine transition policy committees, totaling 135 people, GoLocalProv reports. The announcement followed Wednesday’s rollout of 11 transition co-chairs. Transition executive director June Rose said the committees’ job is to turn the campaign’s affordability agenda into “changes they can feel in their daily lives” and to “think boldly and bring forward recommendations the administration can put to work.”

The co-chairs are heavy on elected officials, city staff, nonprofits and a union political director. They include City Council President Rachel Miller; Ward 3 Councilor Sue AnderBois; former state representative Aaron Regunberg; Gonzalo Cuervo of the General Treasurer’s office; SEIU 1199 political director Alex Moore; Providence Police Lt. Terrence L. Green; and nonprofit and city-culture figures Charlotte Abotsi, Stephanie Gonzalez, Lisa Pina-Warren, Wole T. Akinbi and Chanda Womack, per GoLocal’s list.

Who is missing. GoLocal’s comparison is plain: “Unlike previous mayoral transition teams, Morales has no appointed members from major employers, financial services firms, or college and university administrators.” An editor’s note adds: “Morales did not include leaders from the City’s largest businesses or from the colleges and universities.” On Wednesday, after the co-chair list dropped with no business seats, Rose told GoLocal small-business committees were coming. Thursday’s list does include Small Business subcommittees (nighttime economy, small-business support, hospitality and tourism) with local venues, RIBBA’s Lisa Ranglin, Rhode Island Commerce’s Doris S. Blanchard, and the Providence Marriott Downtown’s Todd Lincoln, among others. That is not the same as putting a large employer, a bank, or a university administrator at the co-chair table. GoLocal notes prior transitions under Angel Taveras and Jorge Elorza had business leaders among the chairs.

The Providence Eye quotes Morales: “In the weeks ahead, our transition team will be hard at work, preparing bold policy proposals and budgetary ideas.” Miller said the team is “tapping seasoned talent that represents decades of experience in service, business, nonprofits, unions.”

Fact check: (1) The 135 figure and the nine committees come from Morales’s Thursday announcement as reported by GoLocal and The Providence Eye. Committee members are volunteers. (2) “No major employers, financial services firms, or college and university administrators” is GoLocal’s reporting, not our invention. Small-business and hospitality names do appear on the subcommittee lists; we are not claiming the transition has zero private-sector people. (3) We are not claiming wrongdoing, only documenting who was invited to shape the first-term agenda and who was left out. (4) Whether any transition volunteer later takes a city job is unanswered; GoLocal asked and did not get a clear bar.

Virtue Forge commentary

A transition team is a preview of who will have the mayor’s ear. Packing it with councilors, former statewide candidates, a union political director and advocacy nonprofits, while leaving off the city’s largest employers and universities, is a choice. Small nightclubs and a hospitality subcommittee are not a substitute for the institutions that employ thousands of Providence workers and pay a large share of the tax base those affordability plans will lean on.

Prior mayors put business chairs at the top of the transition. Morales put politicians and activists there, then added small-business names one layer down. That is how a city government narrows who counts as “the community.” Affordability talk that excludes major employers and colleges is easy to campaign on and hard to deliver without the people who write the biggest checks and hire the biggest workforces.

Bias check: Virtue Forge wants private enterprise and universities in the room when a city sets tax, housing and labor policy. To be fair, Rose promised small-business seats and delivered subcommittee slots, and volunteers from local venues and RIBBA are real business voices. The news is the continued absence of major employers and higher-ed administrators from the top of the stack, documented by GoLocal against recent Providence transitions.

What to watch

• Whether any large-employer or university names are added before inauguration.
• Whether transition volunteers are barred from city jobs, a question GoLocal raised and did not get answered.
• The first budget and housing proposals that come out of these committees.
