Providence Is Paying Its Departing Schools Chief Nearly $200,000 to Leave Mid-Year, With a Non-Disparagement Clause and a Deal Negotiated by a Former School Board Member

Virtue Forge briefing · Nov. 17, 2026 · Based on Boston Globe and NBC 10 (WJAR) reporting on the separation agreement, plus Rhode Island Current and earlier GoLocalProv reporting

What happened

The exit terms Providence wouldn’t release last month are out. Superintendent Javier Montañez, who announced his resignation Oct. 30, is in his last week on the job, and under a separation agreement obtained by the Boston Globe’s Steph Machado and by NBC 10, he will be paid close to $200,000 on his way out.

According to the documents NBC 10 obtained, Montañez will be paid $152,706.92 in biweekly installments, with the final payment scheduled for July 2, plus $46,968.35 for 54.25 days of unused vacation. The Globe reported that the $152,706 is “essentially his remaining salary for this school year,” and that he’ll “keep his city-sponsored health insurance until next summer.”

Two more details from the Globe: the deal “includes a non-disparagement clause,” and it “was negotiated by Lenny Lopes, a prominent lawyer and lobbyist who used to serve on the Providence School Board.” The agreement still needs the School Board’s approval at its meeting Wednesday, Nov. 18, which is also Montañez’s last day.

His replacement is already lined up. The board appointed Chief of Schools Lendozia Edwards interim superintendent, effective Thursday, Nov. 19, through June 30, 2028. Rhode Island Current reported she will be the first Black woman to lead the district. Her salary has not been reported. NBC 10 reports that the agreement says Montañez “will remain available to assist” Edwards through June 30.

Fact check: (1) Not final yet. This is a proposed agreement. The board hadn’t voted on it as of this writing. (2) Is it “severance” or salary? Both, depending on how you count. The Globe calls the $152,706 severance and says it is essentially the rest of this year’s pay. NBC 10 notes he is to stay available to help the interim chief through June, so the district will argue it’s getting something for the money. The agreement as reported doesn’t say how many hours, if any, that help requires. (3) The vacation payout is for leave he has already accrued, and payouts like this are common in public contracts. It isn’t a new gift, but it is part of what taxpayers will pay. (4) “Golden parachute” is the Globe’s headline, not our phrase. For context, WPRI has reported that his July 2024 contract was a three-year, $200,000 deal with 3% annual raises, and the Globe has reported it runs through June 30, 2027. So the payout tracks his contract. It isn’t a bonus on top of it. (5) Lopes is a private lawyer who represented Montañez. Nothing reported suggests he did anything improper. We note his past board seat because it shows how small the circle negotiating these deals is.

Virtue Forge commentary

In the private sector, an executive who walks out mid-year usually walks away from the rest of his pay. In Providence, the departing superintendent will collect the rest of the school year’s salary, plus his vacation, plus city health coverage into next summer. Meanwhile, the district also has to pay a new interim chief. Taxpayers are covering two superintendents for the rest of the year, and the city’s schools are just four months out of state control.

Then there’s the gag. A non-disparagement clause in a public separation agreement means the public’s own officials agree not to say what went wrong. As we reported in October, this exit came during a leadership exodus, a school board fighting over its own president, and four schools on space heaters. When the district refused to release the terms last month, it said the deal wasn’t finalized. Now we know what was in it: a year’s pay, and a promise of silence.

And it was negotiated by a former member of the same board on the other side of the table. That isn’t illegal, and Lopes was doing his job for his client. But it’s a picture of how Rhode Island works. The people who sit on public boards come back as the lawyers and lobbyists who negotiate with them, and the public sees the deal only after reporters get their hands on it.

Bias check: Virtue Forge is skeptical of the political class that runs Providence and of public contracts that guarantee pay regardless of results. To be fair, buying out the rest of a contract is a common and often cheaper way to part with an executive than litigating, the board has a right to settle, and Montañez’s defenders credit him with higher graduation rates and lower chronic absenteeism. Our objection is to the secrecy, the gag clause, and a system where the taxpayer pays full freight either way.

What to watch

• Whether the School Board approves the agreement Wednesday as written, and whether any member objects to the non-disparagement clause.
• Whether the district posts the signed agreement as a public record.
• Edwards’s salary as interim superintendent.
• What “remain available to assist” means in practice between now and June 30.
