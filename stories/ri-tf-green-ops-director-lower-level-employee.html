Rhode Island

T.F. Green Called a Worker Arrested With a Gun a “Lower-Level Contract Employee.” Records Show He Was the Airport’s New Director of Operations, Paid Up to $120K Plus Relocation

Virtue Forge briefing · Oct. 30, 2026 · Based on NBC 10 (WJAR) I-Team reporting from public records and WPRI 12 News reporting from the police report

What happened

On Oct. 2, airport police at Rhode Island T.F. Green International Airport arrested Nathan Brown, 48, after a random security check turned up an unloaded pistol in his backpack as he came in to work. According to WPRI 12, which cited the police report, the gun was a .40-caliber Glock 22. Officers found no ammunition but did find a knife with a 4-inch blade. Brown was charged with possession of a firearm without a permit and possession of a weapon other than a firearm. He holds a Washington state concealed-carry permit, which is not valid in Rhode Island. Brown was later fired.

The same day, outside airport spokesman Bill Fischer opened the airport’s statement by calling Brown “a new, lower-level contract employee,” NBC 10’s I-Team reported. The statement also said the Transportation Security Administration had identified the gun and that security protocols worked as intended.

What the records show

Records NBC 10 obtained through a public records request show that Brown had been hired as the airport’s director of operations. The job paid up to $120,000 a year, and the airport approved up to $10,000 to move him from the West Coast. NBC 10 reported that the job’s duties included senior-level operations leadership and supervision.

NBC 10 also reported that internal records show an airport executive vice president wrote in early June that she needed to fill the position “asap.” Brown’s résumé listed construction management as his most recent work, and his last airport job had been six years earlier, in Seattle. Even so, a mid-August email announcing his arrival credited him with “extensive airport operations experience.”

WPRI reported the same title earlier this month. Brown’s attorney, John Calcagni, told 12 News that Brown was hired as the airport’s director of operations and had moved his family from Washington on Sept. 6, two days before his first day. At that point, according to WPRI, Fischer said only that Brown was hired as a contract worker about a month earlier. WPRI said it asked Fischer about Brown’s job title and had not heard back.

The airport’s response

When NBC 10 went back to Fischer with the records, he said Brown had been hired through a subcontractor. He pointed to the airport’s 2,000-person workforce and said directors sit several levels below executive leadership at the Rhode Island Airport Corporation (RIAC). “There appears to be some misunderstanding about the title of director,” Fischer wrote. He laid out RIAC’s ranks: president and CEO, then executive vice presidents, senior vice presidents, vice presidents, assistant vice presidents, directors, and assistant directors.

“Our earlier reference to Mr. Brown as a lower-level contract employee was intended to describe his position relative to RIAC’s executive management, not to dispute his professional title, qualifications or compensation,” Fischer wrote. On the arrest, he said the airport followed its established security procedures and that “The matter is now before the court, and it is for the judicial process to determine the legal issues and circumstances surrounding the arrest.”

Fact check: (1) Title: Confirmed. NBC 10 has the public records, WPRI separately reported it, and the airport now says it is “not to dispute his professional title, qualifications or compensation.” (2) Pay and relocation: The $120,000 ceiling and $10,000 relocation figures come only from records NBC 10 obtained. We have not seen the documents ourselves. (3) The charges are allegations. Brown has not been convicted and is presumed innocent. WPRI reported that he was released on $10,000 personal recognizance and is due back in court Jan. 4, 2027. (4) The two accounts of the arrest differ. The arrest report says Brown answered the random check with “I need to drop something off first” and disclosed the pistol after being told his bag had to be searched. Calcagni told NBC 10 that Brown first notified his supervisors that he had mistakenly brought a gun: “I think my client did the absolute right thing.” Calcagni told WPRI the conduct “was more the product of negligence rather than criminal intent.” (5) Contractor: According to the airport, Brown was hired through a subcontractor. Neither outlet has named the firm.

Virtue Forge commentary

The gun charge is a matter for the court. Our concern is what the airport told the public. RIAC operates the state’s main airport. On the day of the arrest, its spokesman chose to describe the man in charge of airport operations, a job paying up to $120,000 plus moving costs, as “a new, lower-level contract employee.” That description made the story smaller. It took a public records request to show who he actually was.

The explanation that came weeks later, that “lower-level” meant lower than the executive suite, doesn’t hold up. By that logic, nearly everyone at RIAC except a few executives is “lower-level.” The other half of the label, “contract employee,” raises its own question. If a senior operations leader at a public airport can be hired through a subcontractor, the public should be told who that contractor is, what it is paid, and who checked the hire’s background. NBC 10’s records describe a position the airport wanted filled “asap” and a hire announced as having “extensive airport operations experience” even though his last airport job was six years earlier.

Bias check: Virtue Forge is skeptical of how quasi-public agencies handle public information. To be fair, the screening system caught the gun, Brown was fired. Brown’s lawyer says this was a mistake, not a plot. Nothing reported suggests any danger to travelers or that Brown was hired through favoritism. The criticism here is about candor and vetting, not about Brown’s guilt.

What to watch

• The name of the subcontractor that employed Brown, and what RIAC pays it.
• Whether RIAC has filled the director of operations job, and how it vetted that hire.
• Whether the Attorney General’s office pursues the felony charge, with Brown due back in court Jan. 4, 2027.
