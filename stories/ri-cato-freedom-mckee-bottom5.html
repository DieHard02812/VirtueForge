Rhode Island

Bottom Five in America: Cato’s First-Ever Freedom Grades for Governors Put Dan McKee Near Dead Last, and Rhode Island Ties for Worst in the Nation on School Choice

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on the Cato Institute’s “Freedom in the 50 States” 2026 edition (Oct. 22, 2026) and Scott Lincicome’s accompanying commentary, also published by The Washington Post

What the report found

On Oct. 22 the libertarian Cato Institute released the 2026 edition of its “Freedom in the 50 States” index. It scores every state on 97 policy variables in 10 categories of economic and personal freedom: fiscal, housing, occupational, tech and consumer, education, speech, medicine, firearms, policing and recreation. For the first time, it also grades all 50 sitting governors on how they have moved economic and personal liberty in their states.

Rhode Island’s governor did badly. In Cato’s summary, Scott Lincicome lists the bottom-scoring governors as Massachusetts’s Maura Healey, Rhode Island’s Dan McKee, California’s Gavin Newsom, Hawaii’s Josh Green and New York’s Kathy Hochul, all Democrats. National coverage this week focused on Hochul finishing 50th, behind the governors of Hawaii, California, Rhode Island and Massachusetts.

Where Rhode Island stands out

• Education freedom: tied for last. Cato says the bottom states “typically lack school choice programs, have weak or nonexistent charter school laws, and offer few or no open-enrollment options,” and that “Maine and Rhode Island are tied for last among all 50 states” (education category).
• Occupational freedom: F, third from the bottom. Cato ranks Rhode Island behind only Delaware and Minnesota at the bottom. It says the F states have no right-to-work law, a high minimum wage, heavy licensing burdens without universal recognition of out-of-state licenses, and policies that “substantially restrict the options” for independent contractors (occupational category).
• No one aced it. Cato says no state enacted more than 75% of the pro-freedom policies it measured on either economic or personal freedom. Arizona ranks first overall; South Carolina, Alaska, Massachusetts, New York and Hawaii rank last.

Fact check: (1) The source is an advocacy think tank. Cato is libertarian, and its definition of “freedom” (for example, counting a high minimum wage or no right-to-work law against a state) is a value judgment, not a neutral measure. Hochul’s office dismissed the study as right-wing bias. (2) It isn’t only a red-blue story. Democrat Jared Polis of Colorado is Cato’s No. 2 governor, and two of the five lowest-ranked states, South Carolina and Alaska, have Republican governors. (3) Exact ranks. Some secondary write-ups list the bottom governors in a different order. We rely on Cato’s own text, which puts McKee in the bottom five; we don’t state an exact number or Rhode Island’s overall state rank because we couldn’t confirm it from Cato’s interactive dashboard. (4) Lame duck. McKee lost the Sept. 9 Democratic primary to Helena Foulkes and leaves office in January, so this grades his tenure, not the next governor.

Virtue Forge commentary

The school-choice result stings because Rhode Island already has charter schools and mayoral academies, and the state still ends up tied for last in the nation for letting parents choose. That is a policy choice made in the State House, where protecting the district monopoly and its unions keeps winning.

The occupational score tells the same story about work. When a worker licensed in another state can’t count on that license being recognized here, and independent contractors are squeezed by state rules, the state is protecting incumbents rather than workers. These are not mysteries; they are settings on a dial that lawmakers control. With a governor’s race on Nov. 3, the useful question for Foulkes, Block or Guckian is which of these dials they would turn, starting with universal license recognition and real open enrollment.

Bias check: Virtue Forge shares Cato’s preference for school choice, light licensing and limited government, so we find this index persuasive. Readers who value union protections, a higher wage floor or a strong district school system will see some of these “F” factors as features, not failures. A ranking shows what the rules are, not whether residents are happier under them.

What to watch

• Whether any governor candidate commits to universal occupational-license recognition or open enrollment before Nov. 3.
• Charter expansion and moratorium fights at the next General Assembly session.
• How Rhode Island scores in Cato’s other categories once the full dashboard data is reviewed.
