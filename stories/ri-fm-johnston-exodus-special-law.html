Rhode Island

Lawmakers Rewrote a Decades-Old Law for FM in About 30 Days. Now FM Is Leaving Johnston, and the Mayor Heard It From a Reporter

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on Rhode Island Current (Nov. 10 and Nov. 12, 2026; May 2025), The Public’s Radio (2025), and the Providence Journal (Sept. 2025)

What happened

On Tuesday, Nov. 10, FM, the Fortune 500 property insurer formerly known as FM Global, confirmed to Rhode Island Current that it will move its global headquarters from its 93-acre corporate campus on Central Avenue in Johnston to the office tower at 10 Memorial Boulevard in downtown Providence. FM bought that building in February for about $69 million. Renovations are expected to wrap up in late 2027. The company did not say when the Johnston campus will close or how the move will affect its roughly 1,500-person Rhode Island workforce.

Johnston Mayor Joseph Polisena Jr. told Rhode Island Current he learned of the move when the outlet called him for comment. He called it a “corporate sneak attack.” On Thursday, Nov. 12, Polisena said an FM employee told him that she and at least a handful of co-workers had received layoff notices. “This explains why they have been so quiet about it,” he said. He also said that when his office called FM that morning, the person who answered hung up on his assistant.

Not yet confirmed: The layoffs are the mayor’s account of what one employee told him. Rhode Island Current reports that FM acknowledged the questions but did not confirm layoffs or give details, the employee was not available for comment, and the state Department of Labor and Training did not say whether FM had filed any notice. Federal law generally requires 60 days’ notice to state and local officials before mass layoffs or plant closings by employers with more than 100 full-time workers. Polisena says he has received none. Until FM or the state confirms, treat the layoffs as an allegation.

What it costs Johnston

According to Polisena, FM pays Johnston about $1.4 million a year in tangible property tax on office equipment and furnishings. FM’s 2025 real property tax bill was about $1.5 million. Together that is nearly $3 million a year, roughly 2% of the town’s $144 million budget, Rhode Island Current reports. FM is already suing the town in Providence County Superior Court over its 2025 revaluation. It argues that the campus’s $53.4 million assessment exceeds its “full and fair cash value” and calls the method “arbitrary and capricious.” The town denies the claims, and the case is open.

The 2025 favor

In spring 2025, FM CEO Malcolm Roberts met with then-House Speaker K. Joseph Shekarchi and asked for a change in state law. Lawmakers responded with a late-session bill, introduced May 8 at FM’s request and backed by Gov. Dan McKee, repealing a 1984 law that capped insurers’ alternative investments (hedge funds, private equity, real estate and the like) at 10% of total assets. The House passed it 71-0 without discussion, and it became law.

Shekarchi told The Public’s Radio at the time that FM “made it clear that if they could not create a good business atmosphere, they would look for other alternatives.” Legislators said FM was weighing expansion, and Rhode Island Current reported then that FM executives “made it clear they have no intention of picking up and moving from their” Johnston headquarters. In September 2025, Shekarchi said: “We did that within 30 days. … FM Global is very happy. … They’re strongly thinking of expanding and they’re going to expand here in Rhode Island. So when companies need help, we will respond.”

Context check: did FM threaten to leave Johnston, or Rhode Island? Rhode Island Current’s Nov. 12 story says Shekarchi “revealed the global insurance giant had contemplated leaving Johnston without the law change.” In the September 2025 remarks reported by the Providence Journal, Shekarchi said FM was “considering leaving Rhode Island.” FM is staying in the state. Providence is a few miles away, and the company says it is recentering in the city where it was founded in 1835. So the 2025 deal did keep FM in Rhode Island. It did not keep FM in Johnston, and nobody appears to have promised that it would.

Virtue Forge commentary

FM is a private company, and it has every right to put its headquarters wherever it thinks it will do best. That is not the scandal. The problem is the way Rhode Island does economic development: one company at a time, behind closed doors, with a speaker who can turn a CEO’s request into law in about a month while everyone else waits years for relief.

Lifting an outdated investment cap may well have been good policy. Shekarchi said Massachusetts doesn’t have one, and other Rhode Island insurers benefit too. But if it was good policy, it was good policy for everyone, and it should have come through open debate rather than as a quiet favor that ended with “when companies need help, we will respond.” The House voted 71-0 without discussion. Taxpayers were told FM was thinking about growing in Johnston. Eighteen months later, the town that hosts FM’s headquarters finds outrsquo;s headquarters finds out about the move from a reporter and may lose close to $3 million a year in tax revenue.

Bias check: Mayor Polisena is not a neutral source. He is upset about losing a top taxpayer, and his town is in a tax fight with FM. The layoff claim comes from him. On the other side, FM has not explained the timing, its plans for the Johnston workforce, or why executives said in 2025 that the Providence purchase had no bearing on the Johnston campus. Both sides owe the public more than they have given so far.

The broader lesson is about the climate, which Polisena himself pointed to on Tuesday. A state that has to hand out custom legislation to keep its marquee employers happy is admitting that its baseline rules are not competitive. Fix the baseline, with lower and simpler taxes and lighter regulation for everyone, and Rhode Island won’t need to wait for the next company to come asking.

What to watch: whether FM or the Department of Labor and Training confirms layoffs or a federal WARN notice, when the Johnston campus will close, what happens to the property and its tax assessment, and how Johnston plans to fill a hole of nearly $3 million in its budget.
