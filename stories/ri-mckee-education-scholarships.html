Selling Choice for Union Endorsements: McKee Handcuffs Rhode Island Out of Federal Education Scholarships

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on Brown Daily Herald commentary citing June 2026 policy moves (published ahead of the Sept. 9 primary)

What happened

On primary eve, a Brown Daily Herald column by Ethan Canfield argues Gov. Dan McKee reversed a long charter-friendly record—signing a three-year moratorium on new charter schools and cutting the statewide charter cap—while also signing legislation that bars the governor from opting Rhode Island into the federal Education Freedom Tax Credit program without General Assembly approval.

The column ties those June moves to teachers-union politics: after the moratorium, NEARI and the Rhode Island Federation of Teachers endorsed McKee. It cites heavy excess demand for charter seats (tens of thousands of applications for a few thousand spots), an April poll showing broad public support for charter access and opposition to the moratorium, the lawsuit by De La Comunidad Bilingual Public Charter School, and union lobbying against the federal tax-credit scholarships (up to $1,700 of federal tax directed to K–12 support nonprofits in opt-in states).

Virtue Forge commentary

This is not abstract “education policy.” It is a textbook trade: shrink family options, lock the door on a voluntary federal scholarship channel, and cash the endorsement. That is the opposite of humble, trustworthy service—and it is hostile to free enterprise in schooling.

Bias check: The Herald piece is an opinion column, not straight news—and it shows (poll numbers, “selling out students,” Louis XIV jab). Still, the core factual claims are checkable: the moratorium, the legislative handcuff on the Education Freedom Tax Credit, union opposition, and the subsequent endorsements. Don’t let the opinion packaging obscure the policy bargain.

Americans should not need a permission slip from a union-aligned legislature to direct their own federal tax dollars toward tutoring, special-needs services, or other student supports. Forcing Assembly approval when the Assembly has no appetite to opt in is a quiet veto dressed up as process.

Competition disciplines schools the way it disciplines companies. Freezing charters and kneecapping scholarships protects incumbents, not kids. Competence means delivering results families can choose—not rationing seats so political machines stay comfortable on primary day.
