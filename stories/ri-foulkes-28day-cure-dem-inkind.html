Spreadsheet Cure, Party Subsidy: Foulkes’ 28-Day Filing Returns $21,895 as RI Democrats Drop $126K In-Kind

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on R.I. Board of Elections ERTS CF-2 (filed for period Sep. 2–Oct. 5, 2026; due Oct. 6) and prior GoLocalProv / Board of Elections reporting on over-limit donations

What happened

Overnight, the Board of Elections’ public ERTS portal posted Democratic gubernatorial nominee Helena Buonanno Foulkes’ 2026 28 Days Before Election report (period Sep. 2 through Oct. 5, due Oct. 6). The CF-2 summary lists $21,895.00 in Returned Contributions. The expenditure schedule matches that total with 26 disbursements typed “Refund of Contribution,” many dated Sep. 23 and Oct. 5—right after GoLocal and the Board flagged over-limit gifts.

In the same filing, every reported in-kind dollar comes from the Rhode Island Democratic State Committee: two $45,000 “Digital Communications” entries (Sep. 23 and Oct. 5), plus $21,789.27 employee salaries, $9,643.67 and $472.40 payroll taxes and fees, and $4,691.00 rent and utilities. Those six party in-kinds sum to $126,596.34—exactly the report’s line for in-kind contributions. The filing also shows $15,500 in PAC cash (including Citizens Bank PAC, Democratic Governors Association–RI, District 1199 SEIU PAC–RI, and several professional PACs).

Context: GoLocal’s September review put Foulkes over Rhode Island’s per-contributor annual caps by tens of thousands of dollars; a follow-up cited Board campaign-finance staff putting the excess total at $34,445, with the matter open until excess contributions are cured. The campaign had publicly acknowledged about $10,500 in corrections after the Board contacted it. Independent Ken Block called for forfeiture and penalties. As of this morning, the Board’s published Orders page still shows no new forfeiture or fine order tied to the case; the next Board meeting is listed for Oct. 13 at 5 p.m.

Fact check: Attribute $21,895 and the $126,596.34 in-kind total to Foulkes’ ERTS CF-2 itself (summary lines plus matching schedules)—not to a new Board enforcement order. The refund lines are the campaign’s self-reported returns; do not claim every refund is a proven statutory excess without a Board itemization, and do not invent a fine or forfeiture that has not been published. The $34,445 excess figure and “remains open until cured” language come from Board staff as reported by GoLocal (Sep. 24 exclusive), not from a standalone Board order on the Orders page. Party in-kind support is legal when properly reported; the civic point is the scale and timing alongside an unfinished cure, not a free-speech ban on party help. Skip horse-race cash-on-hand scorekeeping; Block and Guckian also filed 28-day PDFs overnight, but this brief is about the Foulkes cure and party subsidy numbers only.

Virtue Forge commentary

Rhode Island wrote a hard $2,000 annual donor cap, then watched its Democratic nominee’s reports fill past that line until a local outlet and the Board’s own finance unit did the arithmetic. Returning $21,895 on the eve of the election window is necessary housekeeping—not virtue. If the Board’s own excess tally was $34,445 and the matter stays open until cured, a partial spreadsheet clean-up is not a closed case. Rules that bite only after journalists scrape PDFs teach campaigns that compliance is optional until someone notices.

Bias check: Contribution limits and party-coordinated support are contested free-speech and party-building policy. Virtue Forge is not pretending caps are sacred economics, and it is not calling lawful, reported in-kind help “illegal.” The charge is narrower: honesty and humility require curing excess promptly and completely, under the same scoreboard for every ticket, while voters see who is underwriting the nominee’s digital shop and payroll in the final stretch.

Free enterprise and free elections both need referees who enforce the posted rules without waiting for a scandal cycle. A state Democratic committee dropping six-figure in-kind—digital communications, salaries, rent, payroll taxes—into the same 28-day report that finally lists five-figure refunds is the machine in plain sight. Rhode Islanders who want trustworthy government should demand a public, line-by-line cure status before Oct. 13, equal enforcement if forfeiture is warranted, and an end to treating campaign-finance law as a suggestion until GoLocal opens the spreadsheet.
