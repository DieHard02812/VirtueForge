Hasbro Makes It Official: 587 RI Jobs Pack for Boston—Proof a $1 Riverfront Lot Is No Substitute for a Competitive State

Virtue Forge briefing · Based on Boston Globe and Ocean State Media reporting (Oct. 13, 2026) on Hasbro’s WARN Act filing with the R.I. Department of Labor and Training, plus Rhode Island Current’s Sept. 8, 2025 report on the original move announcement

What happened

Hasbro, the century-old Pawtucket toy and game maker behind Monopoly, Nerf, Play-Doh and Mr. Potato Head, has filed a federal WARN Act notice with the Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training. The notice, dated Oct. 8, says 587 positions in Rhode Island will be affected effective Dec. 24 as the company closes its Pawtucket site and moves to its new headquarters at 400 Summer St. in Boston’s Seaport District. According to the Globe, Hasbro’s chief people officer wrote that every affected employee was offered the chance to keep their job at the Boston location starting Jan. 4, 2027. Ocean State Media reported that the filing says the company has not yet confirmed how many employees will make the move.

A Hasbro spokesperson told the Globe the filing is “a formal notice of our move to Boston” and “not a new announcement or layoff notice,” noting that employees were told of the move on Sept. 1, 2025. Pawtucket Mayor Don Grebien said it is “deeply disappointing that the company continues to remain silent and unresponsive” to outreach from city and state officials. The state Labor and Training director told Ocean State Media his agency plans a job fair next month for Hasbro workers.

Context: Hasbro announced the move on Sept. 8, 2025. Massachusetts officials had been courting the company since at least April 2024, according to public emails reported by Rhode Island Current. Rhode Island’s main counteroffer was the chance to buy an acre of undeveloped I-195 Redevelopment District land in Providence for $1. Broader tax incentives like the ones Massachusetts pitched were never put before the General Assembly. In September 2025 Massachusetts’ Economic Assistance Coordinating Council approved $14 million in tax credits tied to the relocation. Hasbro said Boston offered talent, academic partnerships and room to “accelerate innovation”; CEO Chris Cocks had also said the circa-1900 Pawtucket building was “showing its age.”

Virtue Forge commentary

First, the free-enterprise point: Hasbro is a private company and has every right to put its headquarters wherever it thinks it will compete best. Nobody owes a mayor a meeting about how a business uses its own property. The useful question isn’t why Hasbro left. It’s why Rhode Island keeps losing the argument.

Look at how the two states competed. Massachusetts spent more than a year courting the company and backed it with $14 million in tax credits. Rhode Island scrambled late with a $1 land deal and a pitch that never reached a legislative vote. Both approaches are versions of the same bad game: governments picking favored companies and bidding taxpayer money for them. Rhode Island lost because it played late and small, not because it refused to play. The real fix isn’t a bigger giveaway next time. It’s a state where taxes, permitting, energy costs and the overall cost of doing business are competitive enough that every employer gets a good deal, not just the famous ones with a headquarters to dangle.

The political reaction says plenty. When the move was announced, Gov. Dan McKee said it “does not impede Rhode Island’s growth or economic success.” Losing the state’s best-known corporate name, and hundreds of good-paying jobs, should prompt some humility instead of talking points. House Minority Leader Mike Chippendale put the better lesson bluntly at the time: “We must stop chasing corporations with taxpayer-funded giveaways, focus on correcting what is driving this exodus, and improve our business climate for those that have invested in our state.”

Fact check: Some coverage says Hasbro “sent pink slips” or is “laying off” 587 people. That isn’t quite right. The WARN notice is legally required because the Pawtucket site is closing, but Hasbro says each employee was offered a job in Boston. How many accept is still unknown, so the eventual job loss to Rhode Island could be lower than 587, or close to it. GoLocalProv also said the move was announced “in March”; Hasbro actually announced it on Sept. 8, 2025, after telling employees Sept. 1. Rhode Island Current put Hasbro’s local workforce at about 1,000 in 2025. The WARN filing lists 587 affected positions, and the reporting we reviewed doesn’t explain the gap. And while it’s tempting to blame Rhode Island’s taxes alone, Hasbro itself pointed to talent, academic ties and an aging building, so the honest charge is broader: on nearly every factor a growing company weighs, the state wasn’t competitive.

What to watch: how many Hasbro employees actually move to Boston, what happens to the Pawtucket Newport Avenue property, and whether the next governor and General Assembly treat this as a reason for structural reform or just another round of incentive deals.
